\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
\begin{otherlanguage*}{english}
The democratization of access to Low Earth Orbit (LEO) via CubeSat-class nanosatellites imposes severe systems engineering challenges. During launch and deployment, the primary chassis is subjected to harsh dynamic environments, including transient shock inside the Poly-Picosatellite Orbital Deployer (P-POD) and random acoustic vibration governed by NASA GSFC-STD-7000A (GEVS). Conventional monolithic chassis exhibit high mass and limited dynamic isolation, transferring critical vibration levels directly to sensitive electronic payloads. This work presents a high-performance computational framework for the parametric modeling, physics-guided deep learning acceleration, and multi-objective optimization of a 1U CubeSat chassis featuring re-entrant auxetic metamaterial panels manufactured via Laser Powder Bed Fusion (L-PBF) in aerospace AlSi10Mg alloy. A 3D parametric CAD model incorporating strict Design for Additive Manufacturing (DfAM) gates was integrated with a finite element-calibrated multiscale elastodynamic model, generating a comprehensive 250-point Design of Experiments (DoE) via Latin Hypercube Sampling. A residual deep neural surrogate model (Physics-Guided ResNet) was formulated with a decoupled $\mathbb{R}^3$ output vector ($\hat{f}_1, \hat{\sigma}_{3\sigma}, \hat{G}_{\text{rms, payload}}$) and deterministic post-inference transmissibility computation ($\hat{T} \equiv \hat{G}_{\text{rms, payload}} / \num{14.1}$), eliminating output collinearity. Regularized by Gibson-Ashby scaling laws and stiffness monotonicity losses, the network achieved a consolidated $\overline{R^2} = 0.9972 \pm 0.0016$ and $\text{NRMSE} = 1.27 \pm 0.34\%$ under 10-fold stratified cross-validation across all 250 samples, alongside a GPU inference latency of \SI[output-decimal-marker={.}]{0.405}{\milli\second} (instantaneous speedup $> \num[group-separator={,}]{200000}\times$ and amortized end-to-end optimization speedup of $\approx 40.0\times$ over 3D FEA). A deterministic state machine built with LangGraph as a strongly typed dataflow graph orchestrated early rejection of non-compliant geometries. Multi-objective evolutionary search via NSGA-II over \num[group-separator={,}]{10000} candidate evaluations mapped the Pareto frontier between structural mass and payload transmissibility. Using the TOPSIS multi-criteria decision method, the optimal flight design was selected (\#28: $\theta^* = \SI[output-decimal-marker={.}]{65.75}{\degree}$ with cartesian deflection $\theta_{\text{def}} = \SI[output-decimal-marker={.}]{-21.40}{\degree}$, $t^* = \SI[output-decimal-marker={.}]{0.613}{\milli\metre}$, node-to-node dimensions $l^* = \SI[output-decimal-marker={.}]{10.42}{\milli\metre}$ and $h^* = \SI[output-decimal-marker={.}]{14.85}{\milli\metre}$, free strut length $l_{\text{free}} = \SI[output-decimal-marker={.}]{6.42}{\milli\metre}$, exhibiting 2D micromechanical Poisson's ratio $\nu_{yx}^* = -12.63$ and 3D homogenized structural Poisson's ratio $\nu_{\text{eff}} = -0.428$). High-order open-source FEA ground truth verification (Code\_Aster) showed close agreement with the neural predictions (\SI[output-decimal-marker={.}]{0.99}{\percent} error in $3\sigma$ peak stress and \SI[output-decimal-marker={.}]{0.008}{\percent} in transmissibility). Compared with the conventional solid aluminum chassis, the optimized auxetic design achieved a mass reduction of \SI[output-decimal-marker={.}]{62.49}{\percent} (\SI[output-decimal-marker={.}]{0.308}{\kilo\gram} $\to$ \SI[output-decimal-marker={.}]{0.116}{\kilo\gram}), passive sub-wavelength elastodynamic vibration attenuation of \SI[output-decimal-marker={.}]{76.50}{\percent} (\SI[output-decimal-marker={.}]{12.6}{\decibel} reduction via local anti-resonances and acoustic impedance mismatch $> 66\%$, invariant under modal damping variations $\zeta \in \{1.0\%, 2.0\%, 3.5\%\}$), a fundamental frequency of \SI[output-decimal-marker={.}]{579.62}{\hertz}, and full compliance with NASA GEVS stress margins and conservative notch Steinberg random fatigue limits ($D = 0.0458 \ll 0.25$), backed by Murakami defect-tolerant LEFM ($\Delta K = \SI[output-decimal-marker={.}]{1.28}{\mega\pascal\sqrt{\metre}} < \Delta K_{th} = \SI[output-decimal-marker={.}]{2.50}{\mega\pascal\sqrt{\metre}}$) across both \SI{300}{\celsius} and \SI{250}{\celsius} stress-relief routes, achieving Digital TRL 4 (NASA SP-6105) and Hardware TRL 3 (ISO 16290:2013) flight qualification readiness.
