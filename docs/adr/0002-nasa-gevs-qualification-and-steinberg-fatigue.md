# 0002. Qualification Criteria under NASA GEVS Random Vibration and Steinberg 3-Band Cumulative Fatigue

Adopt the NASA GSFC-STD-7000A (GEVS) random vibration PSD spectrum ($14.1\text{ G}_{\text{rms}}$, $20-2000\text{ Hz}$) as the governing dynamic qualification baseline, requiring fundamental natural frequency $f_1 \ge 100\text{ Hz}$, positive yield Margin of Safety ($MS_{\text{yield}} > 0$), and a 4x flight life safety margin enforced via Steinberg's 3-band cumulative damage criterion ($D \le 0.25$).

## Status
Accepted

## Context & Problem Statement
Small satellites launched as secondary payloads inside canister deployers (such as the Cal Poly P-POD) endure extreme acoustic and vibroacoustic loads generated by rocket engine combustion turbulence and boundary layer pressure fluctuations during atmospheric ascent.

Without standardized and conservative qualification criteria, lightweight cellular metamaterial structures risk:
1. Dynamic resonance coupling with launcher low-frequency flight transients ($< 100\text{ Hz}$);
2. Excessive stochastic stress spikes at critical re-entrant strut corners causing immediate yield or rapid fatigue failure;
3. Excessive dynamic transmissibility at internal mounting lugs ($T > 1.0$), threatening sensitive payload electronics.

## Decision
1. **Governing Standard**: Adopt NASA GSFC-STD-7000A General Environmental Verification Standard (GEVS) for secondary payloads:
   - Overall Grms: $14.1\text{ G}_{\text{rms}}$ applied independently across all 3 orthogonal axes ($X, Y, Z$).
   - Frequency band: $20\text{ Hz}$ to $2000\text{ Hz}$.
   - Duration: $120.0\text{ s}$ per axis.
   - Spectral PSD profile: $0.013\text{ g}^2/\text{Hz}$ at $20\text{ Hz}$ ($+3\text{ dB/oct}$), flat plateau of $0.080\text{ g}^2/\text{Hz}$ between $50-800\text{ Hz}$, and $-6\text{ dB/oct}$ rolloff down to $0.0053\text{ g}^2/\text{Hz}$ at $2000\text{ Hz}$.
   - Structural damping ratio: $\zeta = 2.0\%$ ($Q = 25$ nominal, conservative $Q = 10$).

2. **Launcher Decoupling Quality Gate**:
   - Fundamental frequency must satisfy $\mathbf{f_1 \ge 100.0\text{ Hz}}$ under clamped rail boundary conditions. (The Pareto optimum achieves $f_1 = 579.62\text{ Hz}$, providing a $+479\%$ safety margin).

3. **Stochastic Stress Quality Gate**:
   - The $3\sigma$ peak response stress ($\sigma_{3\sigma}$, representing $99.73\%$ Gaussian probability) must not exceed the design allowable stress corrected by NASA safety factors ($FS_{\text{yield}} = 1.25$):
     $$\sigma_{\text{adm}} = \frac{\sigma_{\text{yield}}}{FS_{\text{yield}}} = \frac{230.0\text{ MPa}}{1.25} = 184.0\text{ MPa}$$
     $$MS_{\text{yield}} = \left(\frac{\sigma_{\text{adm}}}{\sigma_{3\sigma}}\right) - 1.0 > 0.0$$
   - For the optimal flight chassis, $\sigma_{3\sigma} = 68.8\text{ MPa}$, yielding $MS_{\text{yield}} = +1.56$.

4. **Steinberg 3-Band Cumulative Fatigue Damage Gate**:
   - Apply Steinberg's semi-empirical 3-band technique combined with Palmgren-Miner linear damage summation:
     $$D = \sum_{i=1}^{3} \frac{n_i}{N_i} = \frac{n_{1\sigma}}{N_{1\sigma}} + \frac{n_{2\sigma}}{N_{2\sigma}} + \frac{n_{3\sigma}}{N_{3\sigma}}$$
   - Where cycle distributions follow Gaussian probabilities: $n_{1\sigma} = 0.6831 f_0 t$, $n_{2\sigma} = 0.2710 f_0 t$, $n_{3\sigma} = 0.0433 f_0 t$.
   - Adopt NASA astronautics qualification threshold $\mathbf{D \le 0.25}$ (NASA-HDBK-7005), guaranteeing a $4\times$ fatigue service life safety factor under acoustic random excitation. (The Pareto optimum achieves $D = 0.046 \ll 0.25$).

## Consequences
- **Positive**: Assures full compliance with international launch vehicle providers (Falcon 9, Electron, Vega) and NASA launch services program.
- **Negative / Trade-off**: Demands thorough stochastic frequency response integration (`DYNA_ALEA_MODAL`) and Basquin S-N curve parameterization ($m = 6.8$, $C = 1.2 \times 10^{20}$) rather than simple static g-load approximations.
